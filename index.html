Master's Thesis

Machine Learning Techniques for the Analysis of Qualitative Data: A Case Study

A quantitative study investigating how secondary school students perceive, use and evaluate AI technologies in educational and everyday contexts.

Dataset

  • 1,804 secondary school students
  • 64 variables, 9 thematic sections
  • Provinces of Bari and Taranto
  • Collected within the Italian Scientific Degrees Project (PLS)

Methodology

  • Descriptive and inferential statistics, EFA
  • SEM and path analysis
  • Logistic Regression, Random Forest, GBM, XGBoost, MLP
  • Bayesian Networks, Text Mining

Selected results

  • MLP: 70.3% accuracy, 75.9% ROC-AUC
  • Logistic Regression: a competitive and more interpretable alternative
  • Perceived practical and transformative benefits drove positive attitudes toward AI
  • Bayesian Network scenarios: positive-attitude probabilities from 19.6% to 64.6%

Source code and datasets are not publicly available.

Insurance RAG Assistant

Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Italian motor insurance documentation

A RAG assistant that answers questions on Italian motor insurance documents, combining semantic and lexical retrieval.

Technical details

  • PDF ingestion and cleaning
  • 800-character chunks, 150-character overlap
  • multilingual-e5-base embeddings, ChromaDB
  • Hybrid dense + BM25 retrieval with Reciprocal Rank Fusion
  • Llama 3.1 8B locally via Ollama
  • FastAPI and Docker

Knowledge base

  • 4 policy booklets, the Italian Insurance Code, 2 IVASS/ISVAP regulations
  • 853 pages, 4,874 chunks

Evaluation

  • 38 manually annotated questions
  • Hybrid retrieval with document-name signal: Hit@5 = 0.82, MRR = 0.53

Preliminary evaluation on a small annotated set. Answers may be incorrect despite citations. Local CPU inference takes about 35–140 seconds. Not deployed to the cloud.

Copernicus Earth Observation Data for Wildfire Risk Assessment

Published research · Metodi e Analisi Statistiche (MAS) 2024

Analysis of the spatial distribution and environmental impact of wildfires in Italy during the 2023 summer season, using Earth Observation data from the EU Copernicus Programme (CEMS, EFFIS and Climate Data Store). Co-authored with Crescenza Calculli and Alessio Pollice.

Methods
Rsf · raster · ggplot2Fire Danger RatingFire Radiative PowerBurnt-area analysisK-Means clustering

Main findings

  • Highest wildfire risk concentrated in Southern Italy and the major islands in July and August 2023
  • Sicily and Calabria were the most affected regions
  • The largest fire exceeded 3,100 burned hectares

The repository contains the published paper and presentation material. Source code is not publicly available.
